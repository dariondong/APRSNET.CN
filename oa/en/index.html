About this net

Every Saturday, send your callsign and grid to the bot. The bot answers with an ACK. That is the whole check-in.

Saturday APRS On Air is run independently by APRSNET.CN, with no other organiser in the middle. OA stands for On Air. The bot answers each message with an ACK, and nothing else follows.

There is no sign-up and no list. When the time comes, send the message to the bot and switch the station off. That is all of it.

The net runs on UTC, all 24 hours of the Saturday. Send whenever it suits you. There is no order and no prize for being first.

Both paths count as taking part, and the RF path is the one we would rather hear. When the links around you light up, APRS stands up in that place.

The bot is not online yet. A message sent today reaches nobody.

Time and regions

Every Saturday, UTC 00:00 to 23:59. In local clocks that is one of these rows.

  • UTCSaturday 00:00 to 23:59
  • UTC+8 (China, Singapore)Saturday 08:00 to Sunday 07:59
  • UTC+9 (Japan, Korea)Saturday 09:00 to Sunday 08:59
  • UTC+5:30 (India)Saturday 05:30 to Sunday 05:29
  • UTC-5 (US Eastern)Friday 19:00 to Saturday 18:59

These rows only apply a fixed UTC offset. Where daylight saving is in force, work it out from the offset in that week.

Joining over RF

Both paths reach the bot. The RF one is the one we would rather see.

APRS-IS is the network side: a phone or a computer connects to a server and the message travels between servers. RF is the air side: a handheld or a mobile sends the message out, nearby stations hear it, and a local gateway passes it into the network.

  • Over the network (APRS-IS)Connect to a server and send. No radio needed.
  • Over the air (RF)Send from a handheld or a mobile, then a local digipeater and gateway carry it into the network.

The RF path is what counts locally. When your message is heard around you, other people may switch their radios on. A region with signals all year is a region where APRS stands up. That is the whole aim of Saturday APRS On Air: to get amateur radio APRS growing in each country.

RF transmissions need your own callsign and licence, on the frequencies and paths your local rules allow. To put a local gateway together, see iGate 细则 (in Chinese).

How to take part

Two steps: send a CQ, then send one U once the ACK arrives.

  • Bot nameAPRSNETCN
  • Object nameAPRSNETCN
  • Message prefixCNNET
  • StatusNot online yet, do not send
  1. Step one: send the check-in Send CQ CNNET your info to the bot. Put your own callsign and grid in place of that, as in CQ CNNET BG7XXX from OL62. CQ is the call and CNNET is the message prefix.
  2. Step two: answer after the ACK The bot answers with an ACK, which means the check-in arrived. Send U CNNET to the bot and the check-in is complete.
  3. Optional: silent check-in If you would rather not send a CQ, send CNNET your info straight to the bot. The ACK counts just the same.

The bot is not online yet, so none of the three steps goes through today.

Message formats and examples

Three lines. Fill in your own callsign.

  • Check-in: the call CQ CNNET your info Example CQ CNNET BG7XXX from OL62. CQ is the call, CNNET is the message prefix, and the rest is your own callsign and grid.
  • Check-in: the answer U CNNET Send this line after the bot has answered with an ACK. The check-in ends there.
  • Silent check-in (optional) CNNET your info The same check-in without a CQ. The ACK still counts.

Three things to keep in mind: carry the prefix CNNET in every message, write the callsign that is on your licence, and send one message at a time, waiting for the ACK before the next line.

The message prefix is CNNET, the bot is APRSNETCN, and the object name is APRSNETCN as well. The bot is not online yet. Please do not send.

Questions

The few things people ask first.

Can I check in now?

Not yet. The net is still in preparation and the bot is not online, so a message sent today reaches nobody.

How many steps does one check-in take?

Two. Send CQ CNNET your info first, then send U CNNET once the ACK arrives.

Can I skip the CQ?

Yes. Send CNNET your info instead. That is the silent check-in, and the ACK counts the same.

Does it have to be on the Saturday, and in which clock?

All times are UTC, from Saturday 00:00 to 23:59. Near UTC+8 that runs from Saturday 08:00 to Sunday 07:59, so check the date before you send.

How many times can I check in on one Saturday?

That is not settled yet. It will be in the announcement when the net opens, and nothing counts before the bot is online.

Do I have to sign up?

No. When the net opens, send the message to the bot. There is no sign-up form on this page.

Does anything get counted?

No. This page collects nothing and hands out nothing.

What is the bot called?

APRSNETCN. The object name is APRSNETCN and the message prefix is CNNET. It is not online yet.

What if the bot never answers with an ACK?

Once the bot is online, wait a little and send the line again. The full checks will be in the announcement when the net opens.

Do I need special equipment?

Anything that can send an APRS message will do. A phone with a sound card cable, a handheld with a TNC, or a radio with APRS built in. The 上手与设备 page covers it.

Do I have to use RF?

It is not a requirement, and the network path reaches the bot as well. The RF path is the one we would rather hear.

What if there is no gateway near me?

Without a gateway, a message sent over RF never reaches APRS-IS. Send over the network for now, or look for a gateway close by.

Is this related to APRS Thursday?

No. The net is run independently by APRSNET.CN, and it is not affiliated with APRS Thursday or APRS OTA.

What do I need?

A valid amateur radio callsign and licence, plus a station that can send APRS.
