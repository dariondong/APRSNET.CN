About this net

On the Saturday in Beijing time, send your callsign and grid to the callsign on duty. An ACK counts as taking part.

The net is run by APRSNET.CN. OA stands for On Air.

There is no sign-up. When the time comes, send the message to the callsign on duty.

The net runs on Beijing time, Saturday 00:00 to 23:59. Any moment in it counts.

Stations taking part

One line per station, with its grid, path and time.

  • The list is still empty This round has no station yet.

The earlier rounds and the totals are on the data panel.

How to take part

Send a CQ, then one U once the ACK arrives.

  • Callsign on duty
  • Message prefixCNNET
  • StatusSaturday check-in
  1. Step one: send a CQ CNNET Start a new APRS message and put the callsign on duty in the to field. Write CQ CNNET callsign grid in the body, as in CQ CNNET BG7XXX OL62. A grid is 4 or 6 characters. Without one the station answers asking for the grid.
  2. Step two: send one more after the ACK The duty station answers a CQ with an ACK. Send another message to the same callsign once it arrives, with U CNNET in the body. No callsign or grid this time.
  3. Optional: a line of your own Write it after the grid, as in CQ CNNET BG7XXX OL62 on the hill this weekend. That station gets one more Message line in the roster.
  4. Optional: no CQ Send CNNET BG7XXX OL62 straight away. The ACK counts the same.

Time and regions

The net is counted in Beijing time, Saturday 00:00 to 23:59. Other clocks are below.

  • Beijing time (UTC+8)Saturday 00:00 to 23:59
  • UTCFriday 16:00 to Saturday 15:59
  • UTC+9 (Japan, Korea)Saturday 01:00 to Sunday 00:59
  • UTC+5:30 (India)Friday 21:30 to Saturday 21:29
  • UTC-5 (US Eastern)Friday 11:00 to Saturday 10:59

These rows use fixed offsets. Where daylight saving applies, work it out from that week.

Joining over RF

Both paths reach the duty station. RF is better.

The network side is APRS-IS: a phone or a computer connects to a server and sends. The air side is RF: a handheld or a mobile sends the message, nearby stations hear it, and a gateway passes it into the network.

  • Over the network (APRS-IS)Connect to a server and send. No radio needed.
  • Over the air (RF)Send from a handheld or a mobile, then a local digipeater and gateway carry it into the network.

Transmissions need your own callsign and licence, on the frequencies and paths your local rules allow. To set up a gateway, see iGate 细则 (in Chinese).

Message formats and examples

Four lines. Fill in your own callsign.

  • Check-in: the call CQ CNNET callsign grid As in CQ CNNET BG7XXX OL62. CNNET is the prefix.
  • Check-in: the answer U CNNET Send this line after the duty station has answered with an ACK. Those two words are the whole body.
  • Silent check-in (optional) CNNET callsign grid The same check-in with the opening CQ left out. The ACK still counts.
  • A line of your own (optional) CQ CNNET callsign grid note As in CQ CNNET BG7XXX OL62 on the hill this weekend. The station carries it as its Message line.

Write the callsign on your licence. A grid is 4 or 6 characters; a check-in without one comes back asking for the grid, so send it again.

Independence statement

  • The net is run independently by APRSNET.CN. It is not affiliated with the official APRS Thursday or APRS OTA.
  • There are no scores, no badges and no leaderboard.
  • Every transmission must use a valid amateur radio callsign and a valid licence.
  • The net must not be used for commercial gain, and it must not transmit broadcast-type signals.
